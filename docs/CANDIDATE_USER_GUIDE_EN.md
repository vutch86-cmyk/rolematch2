# RoleMatch User Guide for Candidates

> **Purpose:** Help you understand how recruiters may perceive your CV, identify gaps against a specific job description, and improve how you present your genuine experience.

## 1. What does RoleMatch help you do?

RoleMatch compares your current CV with a specific job description, or **JD**, to help you:

- Understand your strongest qualifications and requirements that are not yet clearly supported.
- See your CV from a recruiter's initial screening perspective.
- Compare your skills, experience, and terminology with the job requirements.
- Rewrite work-experience bullets so they are clearer and better supported.
- Generate an updated CV, compare it with the original, and export it in a suitable format.

RoleMatch helps you communicate your qualifications more effectively. It does not guarantee an interview or a job offer. Every statement in your CV must accurately reflect your actual experience, skills, and achievements.

## 2. Quick workflow

1. Sign in and select the **Candidate** tab.
2. Paste or upload the job description and your current CV.
3. Check the text extracted from your files.
4. Select **Start CV Analysis Now**.
5. Review **Recruiter Perspective** to understand your initial impression.
6. Open **Match Score & JD Keywords** to identify priority requirements.
7. Visit **Update CV** to review suggested rewrites and provide accurate supporting details.
8. If needed, generate an updated CV, verify every change, and export or copy the final version.

## 3. Prepare the job description and your CV

### 3.1. What should the job description include?

Use the JD for the exact position you are considering. It should clearly describe:

- The job title and seniority level.
- Main responsibilities and the scope of the role.
- Required skills, tools, and methods.
- Expected experience or outcomes.
- Which requirements are essential and which are only advantageous.

If the JD is too short, lacks context, or combines several unrelated roles, the analysis may be less useful or harder to interpret.

### 3.2. What should your CV include?

Prioritize information you can explain and verify:

- Job titles, employers, and employment dates.
- Responsibilities you personally performed.
- Tools, skills, and methods you actually used.
- Relevant scope, such as systems, teams, projects, customers, or markets.
- Outcomes, achievements, and metrics you can confidently discuss in an interview.

You do not need to include every personal detail. Avoid sensitive information and details unrelated to the position.

### 3.3. Upload a file, import from a link, or paste the text

You can paste text directly, upload files for both the JD and the CV, or click **Import Link** to automatically extract the job description from a job posting URL (LinkedIn, ITviec, TopCV, VietnamWorks...). If the portal requires you to sign in first, a popup window will open so you can log in safely and use the 1-click AI clipboard extractor to pull the clean JD.

- Common supported formats include **PDF**, **DOCX**, **DOC**, **TXT**, and **Markdown**.
- The maximum size for each file is **15 MB**.
- Review the extracted text after uploading. Complex tables, multicolumn layouts, or scanned PDFs can produce incomplete or incorrectly ordered text.
- If a file does not contain readable text, use a searchable PDF/DOCX version or paste the content directly.

You can also try a sample preset before using your own documents.

### 3.4. Format your CV before analysis

The **Format Standard CV** option can reorganize your CV into a more readable structure. You can switch between the text view and visual preview.

Formatting does not replace content verification. Confirm that job titles, dates, tools, achievements, and metrics remain accurate after processing.

## 4. Start the analysis

RoleMatch requires both a JD and a CV before it can run an analysis.

After you select **Start CV Analysis Now**, the application compares the two documents and presents the results in three areas:

1. **Recruiter Perspective (10s Scan).**
2. **Match Score & JD Keywords.**
3. **Update CV.**

Results apply to the **specific JD currently provided**. If you change the target role or substantially revise your CV, run a new analysis.

## 5. Understand the recruiter perspective

This section simulates what a recruiter might notice during an initial scan. It provides a screening perspective; it is not a real hiring decision.

### 5.1. Interview recommendation

| Result | What it means | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| **YES** | Your CV currently communicates a reasonable level of alignment with the role. | Review the essential requirements and make sure your supporting evidence remains clear. |
| **MAYBE** | Your CV shows potential but still leaves important questions unanswered. | Identify unclear requirements and improve the supporting evidence. |
| **NO** | Your CV does not currently demonstrate enough alignment with the JD. | Review the reasons, reassess the actual fit, and improve only information you can support. |

A **NO** result does not automatically mean you lack the ability to perform the job. Your CV may omit relevant experience, the position may target a different seniority level, or the JD may require skills you have not yet developed.

### 5.2. Six areas to review

RoleMatch summarizes:

1. The interview recommendation and its primary reason.
2. The first impression created by a brief CV scan.
3. Your most visible strength.
4. The main question or concern a recruiter may have.
5. The section most likely to weaken the initial impression.
6. Three improvements to prioritize.

Treat a recruiter concern as a question to answer with genuine information. Do not respond by inventing certifications, experience, or achievements.

## 6. Interpret the match scores correctly

### 6.1. Four score categories

| Score | What it describes | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Overall Fit** | How well your CV aligns with the role being analyzed. | Use it to prioritize improvements, not as a prediction of hiring success. |
| **Technical Keyword Match** | How closely your CV uses relevant terminology from the JD. | Check whether your actual experience is described using appropriate language. |
| **Skill Set Match** | How the skills presented in your CV align with the role's requirements. | Identify which skills are supported and which need clearer evidence. |
| **Experience & Outcome Match** | How well your responsibilities, experience, and achievements fit the position. | Add accurate context, scope, and outcomes instead of listing duties alone. |

The overall score is not necessarily the average of the three component scores. Do not assume a fixed weighting formula or interpret the score as a hiring guarantee.

### 6.2. Displayed score ranges

| Score | Reference label | Interpretation |
|---:|---|---|
| **80% or above** | **Excellent** | Your CV demonstrates several relevant signals, but essential requirements still need verification. |
| **60%–79%** | **Moderate** | There is some alignment, although important sections may need clearer evidence. |
| **Below 60%** | **Needs Work** | Review the gaps between your CV and the JD, or reconsider whether the position is a realistic fit. |

These ranges only provide context for the JD being analyzed. Do not directly compare scores generated for different job descriptions.

### 6.3. When the scores do not align

- **High keyword match but low experience fit:** Your CV may use the right terminology without showing concrete examples, scope, or outcomes.
- **High experience fit but low keyword match:** You may have relevant experience but describe it differently from the JD.
- **High skill match but low overall fit:** The role may require additional seniority, industry context, responsibility, or results that your CV does not currently show.

## 7. Review the CV-to-JD comparison table

This is the most useful section for identifying what needs to change and why.

Each row typically includes:

- A requirement or keyword from the JD.
- The importance of that requirement.
- Relevant evidence found in your CV.
- A gap or point requiring clarification.
- The current match status.

### 7.1. Match statuses

| Status | Meaning | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| **Matched** | Your CV already contains relevant supporting evidence. | Keep the information clear and add context if the evidence is too brief. |
| **Needs Work** | There is some relevant information, but it is not specific enough. | Add genuine details about responsibilities, tools, scope, or outcomes. |
| **Missing** | The system did not find clear supporting evidence in your CV. | Check whether you have relevant experience that was omitted or whether you genuinely need to develop the skill. |

**Missing** means the current CV does not provide enough evidence. It does not automatically prove that you lack the capability.

### 7.2. Prioritize the right requirements

Start with **Very Important** requirements marked **Missing** or **Needs Work**. Then address **Important** requirements, followed by **Nice to Have** criteria.

Use the available search and filters to narrow the view by status, importance, or keyword category.

Keyword categories may include technical skills, tools and methods, desired experience, expected outcomes, and ways of working.

## 8. Turn the analysis into an improvement plan

RoleMatch summarizes:

- Your strongest point relative to the JD.
- Keywords, skills, or experience that are not clearly demonstrated.
- Sections that are vague or overly generic.
- Immediate actions to prioritize.

Work through the following order:

1. Clarify essential requirements you genuinely meet but have not described well.
2. Highlight verifiable responsibilities, scope, and outcomes.
3. Use the JD's terminology only when it accurately reflects your experience.
4. Reduce content that is unrelated to the target role.
5. Review the complete CV for consistency.

You can check off action items as you work through them. Checking an item does not automatically edit your CV.

## 9. Rewrite work-experience bullets responsibly

In **Update CV**, RoleMatch compares the original statement with a suggested rewrite and explains:

- What is unclear or incomplete in the original.
- How the proposed version improves the wording.
- Why the new version may communicate your experience more effectively.

A practical structure is:

> **Action + tool or method + scope + genuine result**

For example:

> **Original:** “Managed company IT systems.”
>
> **Clearer version:** “Managed IT infrastructure operations across four Southeast Asian offices, coordinated incident response, and monitored the availability of critical systems.”

Use the example only if those details accurately reflect your own experience. If you do not know an exact metric, describe the scope honestly instead of inventing a percentage or outcome.

You can copy individual bullets or copy all valid rewritten bullets.

### 9.1. Answer clarification questions

RoleMatch may ask short questions about tools, scale, responsibilities, or outcomes.

Provide information you know to be accurate. Then select **Re-analyze with New Answers** to update the analysis.

This is a new analysis request and may use another unit from your daily allowance.

### 9.2. When there is not enough evidence

If a bullet does not contain enough reliable detail, the system may request additional information before offering a rewrite.

Do not fill the gap with unverified metrics, certifications, technologies, or accomplishments simply to make the wording sound stronger.

## 10. Generate an updated CV

Select **Update CV (Gemini AI)** or the CV-generation option in the update section to create a revised CV based on the JD, your original CV, and the analysis results.

### 10.1. Review every change

After generation, review:

- The summary of key changes.
- The formatted CV preview.
- The comparison with your original CV.
- The raw Markdown version, if you want to copy or edit the content.

Always verify job titles, employers, dates, certifications, tools, responsibilities, and numerical claims. You remain responsible for the final document.

### 10.2. Request further refinements

You can provide instructions such as:

- “Clarify the operations management experience already stated in my CV.”
- “Reduce the professional summary to three sentences.”
- “Use terminology aligned with the JD while preserving all factual details.”
- “Translate the CV into English without adding new experience or certifications.”

Do not request certifications, technologies, roles, or achievements that you do not actually have.

### 10.3. Apply or export your CV

Depending on your needs, you can:

- Apply the revised CV to the input field.
- Copy the content.
- Download a Markdown `.md` file.
- Export a Word `.docx` document.
- Export a PDF `.pdf` document.

Applying the revised CV to the input field does not automatically start another analysis. Run a new analysis if you want to assess the updated version against the same JD.

## 11. Copy, print, and revisit your reports

After an analysis, you can copy the report or use the browser's **Print / Export PDF Report** option.

The **History** panel lets you:

- Reopen previous reports.
- Search by report title or related keywords.
- Delete individual reports.
- Clear all history saved in the current browser.

The application currently retains up to **30 recent reports** in your browser. Reopening a saved report does not automatically run the AI again.

This history depends on the browser and device you are using. Clearing browser storage, using private browsing, or switching devices may make previous reports unavailable.

## 12. Understand the daily AI analysis allowance

Your account may be subject to a daily analysis limit. The current default is **five analyses per day**, although an administrator may change the limit or disable it in the system configuration.

The allowance is shared between the **Candidate** and **Recruiter** tabs. If you reach the limit, wait until the reset time shown by the application or contact an administrator.

To use your allowance efficiently, prepare the complete CV and JD before starting. Re-analyzing after providing clarification also uses the analysis workflow and may consume another unit.

## 13. Troubleshoot common situations

| Situation | Possible cause | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| The analysis cannot start. | The CV or JD is missing. | Confirm that both input fields contain meaningful text. |
| A file is rejected. | The file exceeds 15 MB, uses an unsuitable format, or is damaged. | Select a smaller file, use PDF/DOCX/TXT, or paste the text directly. |
| Extracted text is incomplete. | The PDF is scanned, uses multiple columns, or contains difficult-to-read formatting. | Check the original document and correct the extracted text before analyzing. |
| The score is low despite relevant experience. | Your CV does not clearly describe the scope, tools, outcomes, or terminology used in the JD. | Review each requirement and add genuine supporting evidence. |
| A simulated-result warning appears. | The AI service is temporarily overloaded or unavailable. | Do not treat simulated content as a verified assessment; retry when the AI service is available. |
| You cannot run another analysis. | The daily allowance has been reached. | Wait for the reset time or contact an administrator. |
| Your history is missing on another device. | Reports are stored in the current browser. | Return to the original browser/device or export important reports before switching. |

## 14. Use AI safely and honestly

RoleMatch is most useful when important information is clearly understood as:

- **Evidence:** Information present in your CV or explicitly confirmed by you.
- **Inference:** A reasonable interpretation that still needs verification.
- **Missing:** Information that is not sufficiently supported.

Before submitting your application:

1. Do not add certifications you have not earned.
2. Do not invent years of experience, revenue, improvement percentages, or project scope.
3. Do not claim responsibility for work performed by someone else.
4. Do not include keywords you cannot explain through genuine experience.
5. Do not share your CV or analysis reports with unauthorized people.

A strong CV does more than include the right keywords. It helps recruiters understand what you actually did, the value you created, and why your experience is relevant to the role.
